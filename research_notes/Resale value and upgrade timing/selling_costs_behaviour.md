# Transaction Costs, Market Mechanics and Behavioural Factors of Selling Used Consumer Goods (US & Australia)

Research date: 14 Nov 2026. Region and year are given for every figure. "Hard data" = official fee pages, peer-reviewed or large-sample studies, and government statistics. "Commentary" = blogs, vendor guides, and resale companies with a commercial interest. Many fee figures could only be confirmed via third-party sites, which is flagged inline.

## 1. Platform fees and trade-in programs (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
For a realistic net-proceeds model, use these defaults. eBay US costs about 13.6% + US$0.30–0.40 per order on most categories, with payment processing included. Since late April 2026, eBay AU private sellers under AU$25k/yr pay no final value fee; buyers pay a protection fee instead. Facebook Marketplace is free for local sales and 10% (min US$0.80) on shipped sales. Mercari is 10%. Swappa is about 3% plus 3.5% payment processing. Gumtree AU is free to list in most categories. Trade-in or instant-buyback typically pays about 50–75% of what a private sale would bring (the "convenience discount"); that range comes only from commentary sources.

### Cited Findings
**eBay US (hard data, official page, undated but current at time of fetch, Nov 2026)**
- Most categories: final value fee is 13.6% of the total sale amount up to US$7,500, plus 2.35% on the portion above $7,500. — [eBay US selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)
- Per-order fee: US$0.30 for orders of $10 or less, and US$0.40 for orders over $10. — [eBay US selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)
- Payment processing is included in the final value fee, with no separate third-party processing fee. — [eBay US selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)
- Listing: 250 zero-insertion-fee listings per month, then US$0.35 per listing; insertion fees are non-refundable if the item does not sell. — [eBay US selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)
- eBay adjusted final value fees in most categories from 14 Feb 2025. — [eBay Seller Center, Jan 2025 update](https://www.ebay.com/sellercenter/resources/seller-updates/2025-january/final-value-fee)
- Category-specific electronics rates (commentary): third-party guides cite "Electronics 12.9% + $0.30". I could not confirm this on the official page, so the 13.6% "most categories" figure is the safer default. Some guides also still describe a separate 2.7% + $0.25 managed-payments charge, which conflicts with eBay's current page saying processing is included. — [Closo guide](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/final-value-fees-for-ebay-complete-seller-s-guide-2025)

**eBay Australia**
- Pro Starter plan (no subscription): final value fee of 13.4% incl. GST for most categories, charged on the total the buyer pays including postage. — [eBay AU Pro plan page](https://pages.ebay.com.au/about-pro-subscription); [eBay AU Seller Centre](https://sellercentre.ebay.com.au/selling-fees)
- From late April 2026 (trade press summarising eBay's announcement), non-Pro, Australia-based sellers with sales up to AU$25,000 in the past 12 months pay no final value fee. Vehicles and classifieds are excluded. Eligibility is recalculated monthly; sellers over the threshold move to Pro Starter. — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-australia-fee-free-selling/)
- The buyer pays a "Buyer Protection Fee" instead: AU$0.30 per item + 8% of the price up to AU$20 + 6% of AU$20–500 + 4% of AU$500–5,000, with nothing above AU$5,000. It is added to the buyer's price. — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-australia-fee-free-selling/)
- From 1 May 2026, eBay-issued shipping labels are mandatory for most shipped items between AU$20 and AU$5,000. Local pickup and bulky items are exempt. The international sales fee rises from 1.1% to 3%. — [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-australia-fee-free-selling/)
- Motors: AU$66 final value fee + AU$8.80 insertion fee for vehicles (updated 21 Mar 2025). — [eBay AU Motors fees](https://www.ebay.com.au/help/selling/selling-fees/motors-fees?id=4127)

**Facebook Marketplace (US; third-party sources, official Help Center not reached)**
- Local pickup: no seller fee. Shipped (checkout) orders: 10% selling fee with a US$0.80 minimum. The rate went up from 5% on 15 Apr 2024 and is calculated on the total including shipping. — [Value Added Resource, 2024](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/facebook-marketplace-doubling-selling-fees/); [Vendoo](https://blog.vendoo.co/everything-you-need-to-know-about-facebook-marketplace-fees)
- Conflict: one guide adds 2.9% processing on top of the 10%; most do not. — [Litcommerce](https://litcommerce.com/blog/facebook-marketplace-fees/)

**Mercari (US)**
- 10% selling fee on item price plus buyer-paid shipping, applying to listings created or updated since 6 Jan 2025. The separate seller payment-processing fee was removed. The buyer pays roughly a 3.6% protection fee. All from third-party calculators. — [Crosslist Mercari calculator](https://crosslist.com/fee-calculators/mercari); [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/does-mercari-have-seller-fees)

**Swappa (US, electronics)**
- Free to list. The seller fee is 3% of the sale price. Payment processing is separate: PayPal 3.49% + $0.49, or Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 for some sellers. That makes about 6.5% + $0.49 in total with PayPal. — [Swappa fees](https://www.swappa.com/about/fees); [Swappa blog](https://swappa.com/blog/marketplace-selling-fees)

**Gumtree Australia**
- Free to list in most categories. Listing fees apply in a small number of categories (cars, vans and utes; pets; plus jobs, real estate and services according to third-party guides). — [Gumtree AU help: categories with fees](https://help.gumtree.com.au/AU/articles/en_US/KB_Article/Which-categories-have-a-listing-fee-AU)
- Optional promotion packages (Plus, Featured, Premium) are priced by category. — [Finder](https://www.findershopping.com.au/how-to-sell-on-gumtree/)
- Gumtree Pay: 1.5% buyer surcharge, and the help-page example table appears to show a capped seller-side fee (AU$7.50). The table is hard to read, so verify it. — [Gumtree AU Pay service fees](https://help.gumtree.com.au/AU/articles/en_US/KB_Article/Learn-more-about-Gumtree-service-fees-here)

**Trade-in vs private sale (commentary only)**
- Apple trade-in is quoted at about 60–75% of private-sale value. Example: iPhone 12 Pro at about $250–300 through Apple vs $350–400 privately. Another example: iPhone 15 Pro Max at $550 trade-in vs $750–850 privately. — [BankMyCell](https://www.bankmycell.com/blog/sell-vs-trade-in-iphone); [Swappa: Apple trade-in vs sell](https://swappa.com/blog/apple-trade-in-vs-sell); [Underpriced calculator](https://www.underpriced.app/tools/iphone-resale-vs-trade-in-calculator)
- Trade-in quotes can be cut after inspection; an unverified claim says 25% were reduced in 2025. Launch-period carrier or retailer bonuses can make trade-in beat a private sale. — [Gizmogo](https://www.gizmogo.com/blog/?p=36692); [iMoney](https://www.imoney.my/articles/phone-trade-in-vs-resale-value-malaysia)
- AU: retailer trade-ins paid as store credit can be worth about 20% more than cash offers (vendor claim). — [Phonebot AU](https://www.phonebot.com.au/blog/phone-trade-in-vs-selling-privately-australia)

### Inferences
- A sensible net-proceeds model:
  - Local cash sale (FB local, Gumtree, Craigslist, OfferUp local): net ≈ 100% of the agreed price, but time and risk costs are highest.
  - Shipped marketplaces: net ≈ 85–90% of price before shipping (eBay US ~86%, Mercari/FB shipped 90%, Swappa ~93%).
  - eBay AU private seller (2026): net ≈ 100% of the item price, but a higher buyer-side price may lower demand.
  - Trade-in: ≈ 50–75% of the private price, with near-zero time cost.
- The app should treat fees as a configurable table with an "as of" date, because eBay AU and FB both changed structure in 2024–2026.

### Gaps
- Not verified on official pages: Facebook's fee page, eBay US per-category electronics rates, OfferUp shipped fee (commonly ~12.9%, min $1.99), Craigslist (free for most for-sale categories), Back Market seller commission (Back Market mainly sells refurbished stock from professional sellers; consumers sell via its buyback), and current Apple/Samsung/JB Hi-Fi/Best Buy trade-in tables.
- No neutral, systematic study of the trade-in vs private-sale gap was found. All figures are from vendors.

## 2. Asking vs sold price, time to sell, unsold share, shipping, fraud and time cost

### Takeaway
The best large-sample evidence comes from eBay Best Offer, US, 2012–13, covering 88.4M listings. On average, sale price ≈ 83% of list price, falling to ≈ 73% when bargaining happens. First buyer offers average 61% of list. About 80% of listings never get an offer and don't sell. The app should therefore present "expected money back" as a discount to asking price with a probability of sale, not as a certain list price. Fraud risk is real but small in dollar terms per sale. The seller's time should be valued explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Dataset: all US eBay Best-Offer-enabled single-unit listings, 31 May 2012 – 1 Jun 2013. That is 88.4M listings, 25.4M bargaining threads, and 54.8% used goods. — [Backus, Blake, Larsen & Tadelis, NBER w24306 / QJE 2020](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24306.pdf)
- Average list (BIN) price was $95. Average sale price was 83% of list price, and 73% where bargaining occurred. "Almost 80% of listings never receive an offer and do not sell." 26.3% of listings had the price revised during the listing. — [Backus et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24306.pdf)
- The average first buyer offer was $86.60, or 61% of list price. Bargaining succeeded 45% of the time, and threads averaged 1.6 offers. Sellers accepted the first offer 33% of the time, declined 40%, and countered 27%. — [Backus et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24306.pdf)
- Behavioural norm: parties tend to "split the difference" between the last two offers. About a third of interactions end in immediate agreement. — [Backus et al., QJE 2020 summary](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:135:y:2020:i:3:p:1319-1361.)
- Fraud, AU hard data: Scamwatch recorded 6,724 classifieds scam reports with AU$3.76M in losses over a nine-month period (likely Jan–Sep 2024), with fake seller/buyer accounts on Facebook, Gumtree and eBay. Total Scamwatch 2024: 249,448 reports and AU$318.8M in losses, down 33.1% on 2023. Social-media-originated scams: 7,724 loss reports and AU$69.5M. — [Region Canberra / ACCC data](https://region.com.au/accc-and-ato-issue-separate-warnings-over-rises-in-scams/922794/); [NASC 2024](https://www.nasc.gov.au/news/australians-better-protected-as-reported-scam-losses-fell-by-almost-26-per-cent)
- AU online shopping scam losses in 2024 were about AU$4M (AFP) to AU$4.8M (Vic Gov). The figures differ by definition. — [AFP](https://www.afp.gov.au/news-centre/media-release/check-your-online-shopping-safety-list-twice-festive-season); [Vic Gov](https://app.production.vic-gov-au.sdp3.sdp.vic.gov.au/online-shopping-scams)

### Inferences
- Default heuristics: set the asking price at about 15–25% above the target net. Expect an offer around 60% of ask, and expect to close around 75–85% of ask.
- Sell-through is low for "list and forget" listings: in 2012–13 eBay Best Offer, ~80% got no offer. The app should nudge price revisions after 1–2 weeks and assume a meaningful chance of no sale for low-value items.
- Time cost: a reasonable model is about 1–2 hours per item (photos, listing, messages, meet-up or shipping) valued at the user's chosen hourly rate. This implies a minimum worthwhile item value of roughly US$30–50 for local sales. Below that, donate, bundle or trade in. This threshold is my inference, not sourced data.

### Gaps
- No recent (2024–2026) public data on time-to-sell or unsold share by platform. The 2012–13 eBay data is the best large sample but is old and covers eBay only.
- No sourced survey of hours spent per sale was found.
- No sourced US figures on FB Marketplace scam rates were found (FTC data not fetched).
- No data on day-of-week or time-of-day listing effects from a credible source.

## 3. Seasonality of demand and prices

### Takeaway
The strongest and most consistent timing signal for electronics is the successor launch. Resale and trade-in prices for the outgoing model drop about 9–15% within days of an announcement and 20%+ after release, according to resale-industry sources. For iPhones, the best window is 2–4 weeks before the September event. Holiday demand and the January CES and post-Christmas glut are plausible but weakly sourced. I found no credible source quantifying tax-refund-season or moving-season effects.

### Cited Findings
- Commentary from a resale platform: outgoing-phone trade-in prices fall roughly 9–15% within a day or two of an announcement and 20%+ once the new device ships. The best iPhone window is about 2–4 weeks before Apple's late-Aug/early-Sep announcement. — [Swappa: best time to sell electronics](https://www.swappa.com/blog/best-time-to-sell-electronics) (summarised via search; page returned 403 on fetch)
- ecoATM, a buyback vendor, says resale prices dropped noticeably on every major resale site within one week of a flagship announcement. — [ecoATM blog](https://blog.ecoatm.com/best-time-to-sell-your-phone/)
- Rising new-device prices (tariffs, memory costs) firm up used values (Swappa commentary, Oct 2026). — [Swappa: used tech resale value](https://swappa.com/blog/used-tech-resale-value)
- January/CES influx of new tech makes existing models look outdated; this comes from a liquidation vendor. — [Liquidity Services](https://liquidityservices.com/company/blog/top-places-to-sell-your-aging-electronics-fast)
- AU iPhone timing guidance (2026, vendor). — [Phonebot AU](https://www.phonebot.com.au/blog/best-time-to-sell-iphone-australia)

### Inferences
- Encode a "launch calendar" rule in the app: Apple in September, Samsung Galaxy S in Jan/Feb, Pixel in August. Nudge users 2–6 weeks before the expected announcement for the category.
- For non-electronics, sensible seasonal heuristics are outdoor and garden gear in spring, kids' and study items before school starts (US Aug; AU late Jan), and furniture around moving peaks (US summer; AU Dec–Feb). These are unverified with hard data and should be labelled as heuristics.

### Gaps
- No academic or platform data on general seasonality of used-goods prices, the post-Christmas glut, tax-refund effects (US Feb–Mar; AU Jul–Oct), or day-of-week listing effects was found.

## 4. Behavioural economics: why people don't sell at the right time, and what nudges work

### Takeaway
Owners value what they own at about 2x what they would pay for it, and the gap is smaller (but still present) for ordinary market goods. Status quo bias, sunk-cost reasoning and present bias add to this and keep items unsold while they lose value. Nudges help, but at-scale effects are modest: about a 1.4 percentage-point (8%) uplift in US government nudge-unit trials, versus 8.7 pp in published academic studies. The app should expect small, cumulative effects from reminders and framing.

### Cited Findings
- Endowment effect: in Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler (1990), owners randomly given mugs demanded a median selling price of about $7, while non-owners would pay about $3. Trading volume was far below the roughly 50% predicted by the Coase theorem. — [KKT 1990, JPE](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jpolec:v:98:y:1990:i:6:p:1325-48); [Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1991 JEP](https://www.fermatslibrary.com/s/the-endowment-effect-loss-aversion-and-status-quo-bias)
- The 1991 JEP follow-up says median selling prices are about twice median buying prices, and trading volume is less than half of what would be expected. — [KKT 1991 JEP](https://www.fermatslibrary.com/s/the-endowment-effect-loss-aversion-and-status-quo-bias)
- Meta-analysis (Horowitz & McConnell 2002, 201 WTA/WTP ratios): the less a good resembles an ordinary market good, the higher the ratio. Ordinary private goods have lower ratios than non-market goods. The commonly cited mean ratio of ~2.9 for ordinary goods could NOT be verified here. — [Horowitz & McConnell 2002, JEEM](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/10/703853.htm)
- Tunçel & Hammitt (2014) meta-analysis confirms the gap is smaller for ordinary private goods and smaller still with incentive-compatible elicitation. — [Tunçel & Hammitt 2014, JEEM](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jeeman:v:68:y:2014:i:1:p:175-187); [TSE summary](https://www.tse-fr.eu/articles/new-meta-analysis-wtpwta-disparity)
- Status quo bias: Samuelson & Zeckhauser (1988) found significant status quo bias, stronger when preferences are weak and when there are more options. A 2021 replication supported it in 3 of 4 scenarios. — [Samuelson & Zeckhauser 1988](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/rzeckhauser/files/status_quo_bias_in_decision_making.pdf); [Xiao et al. replication](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2470)
- Nudge effectiveness:
  - Mertens et al. (2022, PNAS, >200 studies): d = 0.43–0.45. — [PNAS critique and data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9351519/)
  - Bias-corrected re-analysis: d = 0.08–0.31, possibly near zero. — [PNAS critique and data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9351519/); [Maier et al. rejoinder](https://bayesianspectacles.org/rejoinder-no-evidence-for-nudging)
  - DellaVigna & Linos (2022, Econometrica; 126 RCTs, 23M people): nudge-unit average effect +1.4 pp (8.0% relative), versus +8.7 pp (33.4%) in academic papers. About 70% of the gap is due to publication selection. — [DellaVigna & Linos summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/347454)

### Inferences
- Endowment effect: the app should show an objective market value (recent sold comps, net of fees) next to the user's own estimate, to correct owner overvaluation. Expect owners' "reservation prices" to start well above market.
- Loss-framing: "this is losing ~$X per month" uses loss aversion in the user's favour, since the loss of value by waiting becomes the salient loss. This is consistent with prospect theory, but I did not find a field study specific to resale.
- Sunk cost (e.g. "I paid $1,200 so I won't sell for $400"): show the decision as forward-looking, comparing value now vs value in 6 months, and avoid anchoring on the purchase price.
- Status quo bias and present bias: use defaults and pre-commitment. Examples are a default "sell-by" date set at item entry, scheduled reminders, and one-tap pre-filled listings that cut friction.
- Expect modest at-scale uplifts in line with the 1–2 pp nudge-unit evidence. Reducing friction is likely the biggest lever.

### Gaps
- Arkes & Blumer (1985) sunk-cost study details were not retrieved.
- No studies on procrastination specific to decluttering or selling, or on marketing-driven upgrade cycles, were found in this pass.
- Exact KKT 1990 dollar figures ($7.12 / $2.87 as often quoted) were not verifiable; the source PDF is a scanned image. Rounded "about $7 vs about $3" is used.

## 5. Value left on the table (unused items at home)

### Takeaway
Industry surveys consistently estimate thousands of dollars of sellable but unused goods per household. In Australia, Gumtree's surveys show roughly AU$5,200–7,000 per household across editions, with the latest report showing about 21 items worth around AU$6,964. In the US, eBay's 2020 survey found 36 items worth US$3,675. These are vendor surveys with an interest in high numbers, and they measure *claimed* resale value, not net proceeds.

### Cited Findings
- Gumtree "Trading in the Circular Economy" report (year not stated; compares with 2021, likely 2023/24): average AU household has about 21 sellable items worth about AU$6,964. The total second-hand market is AU$62B, up from AU$48B in 2021. — [Savings.com.au](https://www.savings.com.au/news/gumtree-pre-loved-items-worth-small-fortune)
- Earlier Gumtree editions: about 25 items worth more than AU$5,200 (2016); about AU$5,400 (2017); AU$34B second-hand economy (2018). — [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/gumtree-second-hand-economy-report/); [Bendigo Advertiser](https://www.bendigoadvertiser.com.au/story/4945007/average-household-has-5400-worth-of-unwanted-items-they-could-sell-online-report-finds/?cs=24); [ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/australian-second-hand-economy-hits-34b/)
- Clothing, shoes and accessories made up 65% of unwanted items in one Gumtree edition. — [Green Moxie](https://greenmoxie.com/report-households-hoarding-3000-in-unwanted-items/)
- eBay Recommerce Report 2020 (US): average of 36 resellable household items worth US$3,675. — [eBay Recommerce Report 2020](https://static.ebayinc.com/assets/Uploads/Documents/eBay-Recommerce-Report-2020.pdf)
- OfferUp 2025 Recommerce Report (1,500 US adults, June 2025): 93% bought pre-owned and 54% sold. Saving money was the main driver for 79%. About 77% of recommerce is outside apparel (2023). — [The Ethos](https://the-ethos.co/93-of-americans-bought-secondhand-last-year-offerup-report/); [OfferUp 2023](https://about.offerup.com/recommerce-report-2023)
- ThredUp Resale Report 2026 (2025 data): US secondhand apparel was US$55.5B, 12% of apparel spend. 60% of shoppers say resale value is a key factor when buying new. — [Retail Brew, Apr 2026](https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2026/04/01/resale-is-taking-a-measurable-share-from-new-retail-thredup-reports); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260402799816/en/)

### Inferences
- After applying the realistic haircuts from sections 1–2 (sale ≈ 75–85% of asking, fees 0–14%, part of it never sold), realisable net value is probably about half of the headline survey numbers. A fair app headline might be "the average household could realise roughly AU$3,000 / US$1,500–2,000". This is an inference.
- "Resale value as a purchase factor" (60%, ThredUp) supports showing projected resale at purchase time in the app.

### Gaps
- No 2025/2026 Gumtree Second Hand Economy Report figure was located. The year of the AU$6,964 figure is unconfirmed.
- No OfferUp or eBay US "unused items value" figure newer than 2020 was found.
- No ThredUp figure on unworn closet value was found.
