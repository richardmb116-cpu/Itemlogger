# Resale Value, Useful Life and Selling Time: Furniture, Power Tools, Bikes/E-Bikes and Sports Gear

Research date: 2026-11-14. Region: US unless stated. Confidence: **H** = primary or manufacturer source, or a measured dataset; **M** = credible secondary source or reseller data with a disclosed method; **L** = marketing or rule-of-thumb claim, a single anecdote, or undisclosed method.

**Bias flag (applies throughout):** Upway, Kaiyo, AptDeco, The Pro's Closet, Peloton Repowered and resale "flipping" blogs all buy, sell or take a cut of used goods. Their depreciation figures are unaudited and serve their own interests. Trade-in buyers want lower values; marketplaces want listings to look like they hold value.

---

## Q1. Used price as % of new by age, and does value collapse near end of life?

### Takeaway
Most categories lose 30–50% in year one and then 5–20% of remaining value a year. Exceptions: premium office chairs and designer or mid-century furniture keep about 70–85% of retail, and mattresses have effectively no resale. E-bikes lose value fastest (about 15–30% of the new price remains at year 5), because the battery sets a hard end of life. Hard, dated age-curve datasets exist only for mountain bikes (2017) and e-bikes (Upway, 2026, method undisclosed). For the other categories, figures are listing snapshots or rules of thumb.

### Cited Findings

**Bikes (non-electric)**
- Mountain bikes, US, 2017 (≈100 completed eBay auctions, Jan 2017, Trek, Specialized, Giant and Santa Cruz, cross-checked against Bicycle Blue Book): about 45% below MSRP after year 1 (brands ranged 44–46%), about 52% after year 2, about 59% after year 3. That is a further 7 percentage points a year after year 1. No reliable data past year 4; the author expects a sharp drop from outdated standards and wear. Confidence **M** (small sample, dated, but measured). — [Singletracks 2017, updated 2024](https://www.singletracks.com/mtb-gear/new-mountain-bikes-lose-about-45-of-their-value-after-year-one/)
- Bicycle Blue Book condition curve (same article): year-1 loss is about 38% in "excellent" condition and about 62% in "fair". Then about 5–7% more from year 1 to 2, and about 2–4% more from year 2 to 3. Confidence **M**. — [Singletracks](https://www.singletracks.com/mtb-gear/new-mountain-bikes-lose-about-45-of-their-value-after-year-one/)
- The Pro's Closet, a competitor, says Bicycle Blue Book does not disclose how often it updates and has overvalued bikes after sudden market drops, so it lags in downturns. Confidence **L/M** (competitor bias). — [The Pro's Closet](https://www.theproscloset.com/blogs/news/how-much-is-my-bike-worth)

**E-bikes**
- Upway's cumulative value-loss table (US, published 2026-09-04, no sources given, claims 100,000+ sales behind its estimator). Confidence **L/M**. — [Upway 2026](https://upway.co/blogs/news/annual-electric-bike-depreciation-rate)

  | Age | Value lost | Remaining (implied) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Day 1 | 20–30% | 70–80% |
  | Year 1 | 30–50% | 50–70% |
  | Year 2 | 45–60% | 40–55% |
  | Year 3 | 55–70% | 30–45% |
  | Year 4 | 65–80% | 20–35% |
  | Year 5 | 70–85% | 15–30% |

  After year 1, value falls about 10–20% of current value a year. The same article's text says "15–50%" remains at year 5, which contradicts its table, and calls keeping 50% at year 5 rare.
- Upway's Belgian site gives about 40–48% of original price remaining at year 3. Confidence **L**. — [Upway BE](https://en.upway.be/blogs/news/quelle-est-la-valeur-d-un-velo-electrique-apres-3-ans)
- Upway trade-in offers range from 15% to 70% of retail and average 34%. Lesser-known brands lose about half their value in year one. Confidence **L/M** (buyer bias pulls these low). — [Upway via search summary](https://upway.co/blogs/news/which-electric-bike-brands-hold-their-value-best); [Upway trade-in](https://upway.co/blogs/news/how-does-upway-calculate-trade-in-value)

**Furniture**
- Kaiyo's 2021 year-end report, as published by Domino: Herman Miller and Design Within Reach "retain the most value… close to original retail". Resale prices in its examples ran about 72–85% of retail: Herman Miller Eames Molded Lounge Chair $828/$1,095 ≈ 76%; Knoll Womb Chair $2,805/$3,563 ≈ 79%; West Elm media console ≈ 72%; CB2 acrylic console ≈ 85%. Confidence **M** (selected examples, listing prices, platform bias). — [Domino/Kaiyo 2021](https://www.domino.com/content/kaiyo-best-furniture-brands-to-resell/)
- Used Herman Miller Aeron asking prices on AptDeco, 2025–26: about 25–70% of the platform's own "estimated retail", with outliers down to about 11% (e.g. $200 against an estimated $1,805, and $750 against an estimated $1,100). These are asking prices, not sold prices. Confidence **L**. — [AptDeco listing](https://www.aptdeco.com/product/herman-miller-aeron-chair-150)
- IKEA Buyback and Resell, current UK and Ireland pages: 45% of original price for "as new", 35% for "very good" (minor scratches) and 25% for "well-used". IKEA Family members get 15 percentage points more. The 2020 UK launch rates were 50/40/30%. The offer is store credit, so this is a floor value, not a market price. Confidence **H** (manufacturer). — [IKEA UK](https://www.ikea.com/gb/en/customer-service/knowledge/articles/719db9e4-4d8e-460f-9d31-7224188dc863.html); [IKEA IE](https://www.ikea.com/ie/en/customer-service/knowledge/articles/83bb6f0a-bbe0-4b2f-a4db-a9ee706eedb5.html); [Which? 2020](https://www.which.co.uk/news/2020/10/ikea-will-buy-back-your-old-furniture-from-black-friday/)
- A US IKEA store representative said buyback credit is "generally maybe 30–40%" of the original price. Confidence **L**. — [Fox2 Detroit](https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/ikea-offers-buyback-of-your-old-furniture-for-in-store-credit)
- Rule of thumb: price used furniture at 70–80% of original selling price. This is pricing advice, not a measurement, and seems high for mass-market sofas. Confidence **L**. — [Money Pantry](https://moneypantry.com/pricing-used-furniture)
- Mid-century or designer appreciation: Eames 670/671 lounge chair and ottoman sets (1960s Herman Miller) sold at auction for $3,900 (Billings 2022, estimate $2,000–3,000), $4,375 (Billings 2021) and $3,840 (Wright). The chair is still in production; the current new price (assumed about $7,000–8,000) is not sourced here. These are point sales, not an appreciation index. Confidence **M** for the sale prices. — [Billings 2022](https://www.billingsauction.com/summer-2022-auction/p/163-eames-lounge-chair-and-ottoman); [Billings 2021](https://www.billingsauction.com/fall-2021-auction/p/284-charles-ray-eames-lounge-ottoman); [Wright](https://www.wright20.com/auctions/2015/09/eames-design-the-jf-chen-collection)

**Power tools**
- Milwaukee resells on eBay at a typical 55–75% of retail, with M18 FUEL the strongest. A used DeWalt 20V MAX drill kit ($199 new) sells for $90–130, about 45–65%. Confidence **L** (flipping-app blog). — [Underpriced.app](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/power-tools-flipping-guide)
- Condition rule of thumb: "excellent" 85–95% of original cost, "good" 75–85%, "average" 50–75%. This looks generous next to sold data. Confidence **L**. — [Hunker](https://www.hunker.com/13403078/how-to-price-used-power-tools)
- The search tool's own summary of eBay sold-listing snapshots suggested about 50–60% of new for used Milwaukee and DeWalt, and about 53% for one Ryobi tool. This is unverified and **L**. — [eBay Milwaukee category](https://www.ebay.com/t/Milwaukee-Power-Tools/3247/bn_7705342)

**Fitness and sports gear**
- Used Peloton bikes reportedly keep about 50–65% of original value in 2025 (citing "Wellfit Insider", untraceable). Confidence **L**. General home fitness equipment holds only 10–25% (**L**). Social posts show used Pelotons as low as $200, which points to a saturated market. — [Reperch 2025](https://reperch.com/blog/should-you-buy-a-used-peloton-in-2025-what-to-know-first); [Longren](https://search.longren.com/en/posts/6931c82e); [The Clip Out](https://theclipout.com/the-rise-of-secondhand-pelotons-in-los-angeles-a-market-shift/)
- Golf drivers: MyGolfSpy found 2025 top drivers depreciated about 28–29% over the year, using PGA of America Value Guide and Golf Galaxy trade-in values. Confidence **M**. A separate guide gives a rule of thumb of 30–40% lost in year 1 and 50–60% after 2 years (**L**). — [MyGolfSpy 2025](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/heres-how-much-the-best-drivers-of-2025-have-depreciated/); [MyGolfSpy trade-in](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/top-10-highest-driver-trade-in-values-time-to-upgrade/)
- Sports gear older than 5–8 years "often struggles to sell". Confidence **L**. — [ItemValueChecker](https://itemvaluechecker.com/sports-equipment)

### Inferences
Suggested model defaults. These are synthesized from the figures above, are not sourced as stated, and need calibration.

| Sub-category | Yr-1 drop | Annual after | Floor (% new) | Useful life | End-of-life onset | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium office chair (Aeron, Leap…) | 30–45% | ~5%/yr | ~20–30% (used-dealer market keeps a floor) | 12+ yrs (warranty length) | ~yr 12–15, or on a model refresh | M/L |
| Designer / mid-century (HM, Knoll, DWR) | 15–30% | ~0–3%/yr; authentic vintage may appreciate | ~60–75% | Decades | None | M |
| Mass-market sofa | 50–70% | 10%/yr | ~5–10%, can be negative (disposal cost) | 7–15 yrs | ~yr 7–10 | L (no hard data) |
| Flat-pack (IKEA) | 55–65% (IKEA buyback ≈ 35–45%) | 5%/yr | ~10–25% | 5–10 yrs | Disassembly damage | M (floor), L (curve) |
| Solid wood (non-designer) | 30–50% | 3–5%/yr | ~20–40% | 20+ yrs | None | L |
| Mattress | ~90–100% | — | 0% | 7–10 yrs | — | L (gap) |
| Cordless tool, premium (Milwaukee, Makita, DeWalt) | 30–45% | 5–8%/yr | ~25–35% (bare tool) | 5–10+ yrs tool; battery 2–3 yrs / 300–500 cycles | When the platform is discontinued | L |
| Cordless tool, value (Ryobi) | 45–55% | 8–10%/yr | ~15–20% | 3–7 yrs | — | L |
| Road / mountain bike | ~40–45% | ~7 points/yr to yr 3, then faster | ~15–25% | 8–15 yrs frame | Yr 4+ (outdated standards) | M |
| E-bike | 30–50% | 10–20% of remaining/yr | ~10–15% | Frame 8–10+ yrs; battery 3–5 yrs | When the battery needs replacing (yr 3–5) | L/M |
| Golf driver | ~28–40% | ~15–20%/yr | ~10–15% | 5–8 yrs | Each new model launch | M/L |
| Peloton / connected fitness | ~35–50% | 10–15%/yr | ~15–25% | 7–10 yrs | When software support ends | L |

"Collapse near end of life" shows up most strongly in: e-bikes (battery costs $600–800+, which can approach the bike's whole resale value by year 4–5); tools (a discontinued battery platform strands the tools); mountain bikes (standards change, e.g. wheel size and axles, around year 4+); and connected fitness (dependence on the subscription and software).

### Gaps
- No sold-price (not asking-price) dataset for used Aeron, Leap or Embody by age. Chairorama and Madison Seating did not publish depreciation figures in searches.
- No quantified sofa depreciation curve from Kaiyo or AptDeco. No mattress resale data found; the 0% value is an inference from hygiene and fire-safety norms and is unsourced.
- No Bicycle Blue Book or Pro's Closet price-index time series for 2019–2026.
- No ski depreciation data found.

## Q2. Which brands retain value, and why?

### Takeaway
Retention follows brand reputation, parts and repair ecosystems, and platform lock-in more than the warranty itself. Premium chair warranties (12 years) are **not** transferable, so their resale strength comes from the large refurbisher and dealer market and parts availability. Milwaukee and DeWalt hold value better than Ryobi. Big-brand e-bikes with Bosch or Shimano drives hold value better than direct-to-consumer and unknown brands.

### Cited Findings
- Herman Miller warranty covers only "original purchasers who acquire new product" from Herman Miller or authorized resellers; 12-year seating term. Confidence **H**. — [Herman Miller warranty terms](https://hermanmiller.com/warranty-terms); [HM US/Canada PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0435/8032/6038/files/hm_warranty_us_and_canada.pdf)
- Steelcase: "No, the warranty is non-transferable." Confidence **H**. — [Steelcase FAQ](https://steelcase.com/resources/documents/faq-warranty)
- Used Aerons carry only the seller's warranty. Confidence **M**. — [BTOD](https://www.btod.com/blog/buying-used-aerons-online). Remanufacturers such as Crandall Office offer their own 12-year warranty on reman Steelcase and Herman Miller chairs, non-transferable and US only. Confidence **H** (seller terms). — [Crandall](https://www.crandalloffice.com/warranty-returns/)
- In June 2026 Herman Miller launched **Aeron Refurbished**, a manufacturer program that restores pre-owned Aerons for resale in the US and Canada. Pricing was not found. This could set an official refurbished-price benchmark. Confidence **H** (press release). — [MillerKnoll 2026-06-22](https://news.millerknoll.com/2026-06-22-Herman-Miller-introduces-Aeron-Refurbished,-extending-the-life-of-its-most-recognized-design)
- Kaiyo, 2021: Herman Miller and Design Within Reach retain the most value. West Elm, CB2 and Ethan Allen sell fastest (some pieces sell within about 21–25 minutes). Confidence **M**. — [Domino/Kaiyo](https://www.domino.com/content/kaiyo-best-furniture-brands-to-resell/)
- Kaiyo prices with an algorithm based on brand value, condition and market data, adjusted almost daily. Confidence **M**. — [Domino](https://www.domino.com/style-shopping/kaiyo-furniture-report-best-brands-to-sell/)
- Tools: Milwaukee M18 FUEL "holds value exceptionally well" (**L**). — [Underpriced.app](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/power-tools-flipping-guide). Ryobi is the value tier and sells fast at lower prices (**L**). — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1959115/tool-brands-with-great-resale-value)
- Tool warranties: Milwaukee M18 tools 5 years (2,000-charge or 2-year limits on some batteries; legacy batteries 1 year); DeWalt 3-year tools; Makita 3 years on tools, batteries and chargers; Ryobi 3-year 18V/40V batteries. Confidence **M** (secondary, mixed dates; verify with manufacturers). — [SlashGear Milwaukee vs DeWalt](https://www.slashgear.com/1723091/milwaukee-vs-dewalt-warranty/); [SlashGear battery warranties](https://www.slashgear.com/2072168/power-tool-battery-brands-with-best-warranty); [ToolboxBuzz](https://www.toolboxbuzz.com/tool-maintenance/comparing-manufacturers-warranties/)
- E-bikes: Specialized, Aventon, Trek and Giant "always sell for more", and Bosch, Yamaha and Shimano motors support value (no figures). Aventon and Tern are named as higher-retention brands. Lesser-known brands lose about 50% in year 1. Confidence **L**. — [Upway depreciation](https://upway.co/blogs/news/annual-electric-bike-depreciation-rate); [Upway brands](https://upway.co/blogs/news/which-electric-bike-brands-hold-their-value-best)
- Non-electric mountain bikes: Trek, Specialized, Giant and Santa Cruz lost almost the same in year 1 (44–46%), so brand made little difference among the major brands. Confidence **M** (2017). — [Singletracks](https://www.singletracks.com/mtb-gear/new-mountain-bikes-lose-about-45-of-their-value-after-year-one/)
- Eames chairs: value depends on materials, condition and year; authentic Herman Miller or Vitra labels matter, and "Eames style" copies sell for far less. Confidence **M**. — [Dawsons](https://www.dawsonsauctions.co.uk/news-item/what-is-an-eames-chair-worth)

### Inferences
Brand multipliers to apply against the category baseline (not sourced, derived from the above):
- Herman Miller, Knoll, DWR, Vitra: ×1.5–2.0 against a generic sofa or chair.
- Steelcase Leap/Gesture: ×1.3–1.6.
- IKEA: ×0.6–0.8 (the buyback offer is the effective floor).
- Milwaukee/Makita: ×1.1–1.2 against DeWalt; Ryobi ×0.75–0.85.
- Premium-brand e-bikes with Bosch or Shimano drives: ×1.2–1.4 against generic direct-to-consumer.
- Among the major mountain-bike brands: about ×1.0, no differentiation.

### Gaps
- No quantified brand-retention ranking for e-bikes (Upway does not publish one) or for tools (no sold-data study found).
- Steelcase Gesture and Herman Miller Embody resale specifics were not found.

## Q3. Seasonal and launch timing (US vs Australia)

### Takeaway
List seasonal goods 1–2 months before their season. Bikes sell best roughly March–July, golf February–May, skis August–December, and fitness equipment in January. Off-season listings reportedly sell for 25–40% less (low confidence). Golf clubs drop on new-model launches. The bike market crashed after 2021: 2023 was the weakest US sales year on record, and discounting on new bikes depressed used prices through 2024. No Australia-specific seasonal data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Bikes: strongest April–August, or March–July; resale demand peaks May–July. Confidence **L** (reseller blogs). — [AOL month-by-month guide](https://www.aol.com/month-month-guide-selling-stuff-140000914.html); [Underpriced.app summer](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/summer-reselling-strategy-what-sells-best-2026)
- Skis: sell best September–December, or list August–November; equipment sells October–February. Confidence **L**. — [Underpriced.app ski](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/ski-equipment-flipping-guide); [WildSnow](https://wildsnow.com/34106/how-where-and-why-to-sell-your-old-ski-gear/)
- Golf clubs: peak March–May, April–August, or list February–May. Confidence **L**. — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/month-month-guide-selling-stuff-140000914.html)
- Off-season listings sell for 25–40% less. Maintained gear (tuned skis, serviced bikes, regripped clubs) sells for 20–40% more. Confidence **L**. — [ItemValueChecker](https://itemvaluechecker.com/sports-equipment)
- Fitness: Acorns covers the best time to sell a Peloton (the January resolution demand pattern). Confidence **L**. — [Acorns](https://acorns.com/learn/earning/best-time-to-sell-peloton-bike)
- Golf launch effect: the previous model is hit hardest when a successor launches; drivers on roughly annual cycles. Confidence **M**. — [MyGolfSpy price shifts 2025](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/when-to-buy-a-driver-how-prices-shifted-for-the-top-5-in-2025/)
- Bike market crash (US):
  - Per Circana, 2023 consumer bike sales were at an all-time low in 20 of the prior 24 months, the weakest year since PeopleForBikes tracking began in 2015.
  - 2024 was projected at about +9% on 2023 but -22% against the 2015–2019 average.
  - Wholesale inventory was above $765M at the end of Q1 2023.
  - Supplier inventory units fell 47% from March 2023 to March 2024.
  - Retail mountain-bike inventory in March 2024 was 42% below its spring-2022 peak.
  - Discount frequency and depth began easing by mid-2024.

  Confidence **M/H** (trade analysts citing Circana). — [Bicycle Retailer, Vosper 2024-01](https://bicycleretailer.com/opinion-analysis/2024/01/05/vosper-compared-last-year-2024-doesnt-look-so-bad-after-all); [Bicycle Retailer 2024-02](https://bicycleretailer.com/opinion-analysis/2024/02/09/vosper-whats-store-2024-part-two); [PeopleForBikes MTB 2024](https://www.peopleforbikes.org/news/mountain-bike-market-insights-2024); [Outdoor Retailer](https://thedaily.outdoorretailer.com/news/brands-and-retailers/bike-retailers-riding-inventory-glut-as-demand-softens-participation-rises/)
- Heavy discounting on new bikes, "maybe the highest and most discounts… in a long time", made used bikes harder to sell. Confidence **M** (commentary). — [Singletracks](https://www.singletracks.com/?p=642890)
- 2023 counterpoint: Bicycle Blue Book and The Pro's Closet trade-in inventories were not rising significantly. Confidence **M**. — [research.bike 2023](https://research.bike/2023/06/bike-inventories-in-2023/). There were early signs of used-price recovery and normalizing inventory around 2024–25 (**L/M**). — [research.bike](https://research.bike/?cat=9)
- US used road bike average price was 842 (currency unstated, presumably USD) across 48,291 listings as of 2026-03-14. Confidence **L/M** (undisclosed method). — [research.bike](https://research.bike/?tag=used)
- E-bikes kept growing: 454k units in the 12 months to April 2023, against 149k in the 12 months to April 2020. Confidence **M**. — [Bicycle Retailer 2023](https://www.bicycleretailer.com/opinion-analysis/2023/05/13/vosper-2023-shaping-be-year-double-whammy)

### Inferences
- Seasonal multiplier for an app (unsourced default): peak month ×1.0, shoulder months ×0.85–0.9, deepest off-season ×0.65–0.75 (consistent with the 25–40% off-season discount claim).
- Australia and the Southern Hemisphere: flip the months by about 6. Bikes peak around September–January (spring and summer), skis around March–June, and fitness still in January (a calendar effect, though it coincides with summer). Patio furniture peaks about 1–2 months before local summer (US March–May; AU September–November). These are logical inferences only, with no AU data found.
- Value bikes bought in 2020–22 at pandemic-inflated prices took a double hit: normal depreciation plus the 2023–24 discount glut. Model a cohort penalty of roughly an extra 10–20% for those model years (unsourced estimate).

### Gaps
- No Australian seasonal or second-hand price data found; research.bike's Australian tag returned US-only data.
- No measured patio furniture seasonality data.
- No dated analysis of the January spike in fitness demand.

## Q4. Repair vs replace and maintenance economics

### Takeaway
Battery cost is the main swing factor for tools and e-bikes. An e-bike battery ($600–800+) can equal much of a 4–5-year-old e-bike's resale value. Tool batteries are cheaper ($50–120) and are often worth replacing. Premium chairs are worth repairing because parts and a large refurbishment industry exist. Tuning or servicing gear before sale is worth about 20–40% (low confidence).

### Cited Findings
- Bosch e-bike batteries: warranty 2 years or 500 cycles. Bosch states about 1,000 full cycles or 8–9 years of life (UK retailer citation). A road.cc source describes the warranty as 60% capacity at 2 years or 500 cycles but could not find it on Bosch's site. Confidence **M**. — [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/feature/old-worn-out-e-bike-batteries-repair-refurbish-or-replace-292657); [e-bikeshop.co.uk](https://www.e-bikeshop.co.uk/blog/post/tag/400wh/)
- Replacement cost: Bosch PowerTube 500Wh $797.50, PowerPack 400Wh $629 (US retailer); Bosch 625Wh PowerTube about £770 (UK). Confidence **M**. — [ebicycles.com](https://ebicycles.com/?p=9150); [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/feature/old-worn-out-e-bike-batteries-repair-refurbish-or-replace-292657)
- Lithium-ion cells lose about 20% of capacity over 500 cycles. Recommended charge range is 30–80%. Confidence **L/M**. — [Upway](https://upway.co/blogs/news/annual-electric-bike-depreciation-rate)
- Battery condition is the biggest driver of e-bike resale value. Confidence **L**. — [Upway BE battery](https://en.upway.be/blogs/news/comment-la-batterie-influence-la-valeur-de-votre-velo-electrique)
- Tool batteries: DeWalt says typical life is 2–3 years or 300–500 charge cycles, whichever comes first. Confidence **H**. — [DeWalt support](https://support.dewalt.com/hc/en-us/articles/13632015752333-Do-DEWALT-batteries-have-a-lifetime). Quality packs reach 500–1,000 cycles to 80% capacity, and some outdoor-power packs claim 2,000–2,500 (**L**). — [Pro Tool Reviews](https://www.protoolreviews.com/how-long-do-lithium-ion-batteries-last/)
- Replacement 18V/20V packs cost $50–120 (unverified). Confidence **L**. — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1940199/how-long-lithium-ion-power-tool-batteries-last-explained)
- Office chairs: a large remanufacturing industry (e.g. Crandall) rebuilds Steelcase and Herman Miller chairs and backs them with a 12-year seller warranty, which shows that repair beats replace for these frames. Confidence **H** (existence of the market). — [Crandall reman](https://www.crandalloffice.com/remanufactured-chairs)
- Peloton Repowered (launched June 2025): sellers keep 70% of the sale price; buyers pay a $45 activation fee instead of $95. New prices are Bike from about $1,745 and Tread $2,845. Confidence **H/M**. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/peloton-launching-resale-market-used-bikes-treadmills-rcna210631); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2025/peloton-launches-p2p-used-equipment-marketplace-repowered/)

### Inferences
- E-bike rule: if battery health is below about 70% (a common practical threshold per search summaries, **L**), deduct the replacement cost (about $600–800) from the value, or apply about a 25–40% value haircut.
- Tool rule: bare-tool resale is typically below kit resale. A tool platform is "lock-in equity": owning several batteries raises the value of keeping tools on the same platform rather than selling them.
- Connected fitness: platform fees (the $45 activation and the 30% Repowered cut) reduce the seller's net. Model the net to the seller at about 70% of the used sale price on official channels.

### Gaps
- No sourced bike servicing cost figures (e.g. annual tune-up cost) or chair part prices (e.g. Aeron seat pan or cylinder) were gathered.
- No Shimano e-bike battery spec was found; only Bosch was covered.
- No study measured whether a battery replacement raises e-bike resale by more than its cost.
