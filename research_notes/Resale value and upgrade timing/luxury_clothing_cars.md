# Resale value over time: Jewellery & Watches, Clothing & Designer Goods, Cars (calibration benchmark)

Scope note: Research done Nov 2026 with about 20 search and fetch calls. Most figures come from search-result summaries of secondary coverage. Primary pages I fetched directly: the KBB depreciation page and TFLcar's write-up of the 2026 iSeeCars study. WatchCharts pages returned HTTP 403, so watch index figures are second-hand. Confidence tags: **[H]** high, **[M]** medium, **[L]** low. Region is US unless noted. Resale platforms (Rebag, Fashionphile, The RealReal, StockX, ThredUp, watch dealers, jewellers) have a **commercial interest** in reporting high retention or appreciation. Their numbers are flagged "(platform, biased)".

## Key Question 1: Typical resale as % of retail at purchase, 1, 3, 5 and 10 years, by category

### Takeaway
Cars give the clearest benchmark. The decline is front-loaded: about 16–20% lost in year 1, about 39–45% by year 3 and about 42–55% by year 5, after which it flattens. Luxury goods split in two. A small set of scarce, price-controlled brands (Hermès, Rolex steel sports models, Goyard, some Chanel) retain about 90–140% of retail. Nearly everything else (most jewellery, lab-grown diamonds, mid-range watches, fast fashion) loses 50–90% the moment it is bought.

**Fine jewellery: gold**
- Resale is priced off melt value (weight × purity × spot). Typical offers: pawn 40–60% of melt (some as low as 25%), local jewellers and gold buyers 60–75%, specialist refiners about 80–95%. Gold coins and bullion: up to about 98% of spot. All figures come from dealer or seller pages [L/M] — [APMEX Learn](https://learn.apmex.com/investing-guide/selling-gold-and-diamonds-how-value-is-really-determined/); [USAGold](https://www.usagold.com/is-gold-jewelry-a-good-investment-how-it-compares-to-coins-and-bullion/); [GoldSilver](https://goldsilver.com/article/is-gold-jewelry-a-good-investment-what-most-buyers-get-wrong/); [Bullion Trading LLC](https://bulliontradingllc.com/blog/best-place-to-sell-gold/)
- Estimates of the retail markup over gold content range from about 25–50% to 50–300% or more, and none of it is recovered on resale [L] — same sources as above

### Inferences
- Suggested model parameters, flagged as **my synthesis**, not sourced numbers:
  - **Cars (US general):** Y1 about 0.82, Y3 about 0.60, Y5 about 0.55. iSeeCars 2026 gives 0.58; KBB gives 0.45. Y10 floor about 0.20–0.30 (not sourced). AU: about 0.60 at year 3.
  - **Gold jewellery:** Immediate value is about 0.3–0.6 of retail, set by melt × buyer haircut. It then tracks the gold price (can rise) and does not decline with age.
  - **Natural diamond:** About 0.3–0.5 of current retail, with current retail itself falling 10–25% a year in 2024–25.
  - **Lab-grown:** About 0.1–0.2, with little floor. Treat it as near zero after a few years.
  - **Luxury watches:** Rolex steel sports about 1.0–1.3. Rolex dress models about 0.9–1.1. Omega and most others about 0.6–0.75, roughly flat after year 1.
  - **Hermès, Goyard bags:** About 1.0–1.4.
  - **Chanel, LV classics:** About 0.75–0.9.
  - **Other designer:** Not established. See Gaps.
  - **Fast fashion:** Near 0.
- In general, scarce, price-controlled luxury items are worth more than 100% only *relative to current retail*, which itself rises 4–10% a year. Once retail increases and resale costs (fees, see Q3) are counted, real net appreciation is smaller.

## Key Question 2: Which brands and items hold or gain value, the evidence, and the risks of relying on appreciation; segment and brand multipliers

### Takeaway
Value retention is concentrated in a handful of brands with controlled supply and regular retail price increases: Hermès, Goyard, Rolex steel sports, Patek, Porsche 911/Cayman, Toyota and Lexus. Appreciation has reversed sharply in the past: watches fell about 37% from the 2022 peak, Jordan 1 premiums went from 61% to 4%, and natural diamonds fell 23% in 2024. The app should never assume appreciation by default.

### Cited Findings
- **Car segments (iSeeCars 2025):** EVs lose about 59% over 5 years; hybrids and pickups about 40%, against a 45.6% average [H] — [FI Magazine](https://www.fi-magazine.com/news/car-types-depreciate-differently); [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/754580/cars-least-depreciation-2025/)
- **iSeeCars 2026 best five-year retention:** Porsche 718 Cayman 9.6%, 911 11.1%, Corvette 18.7%, Civic 22.9%, BRZ 23.7%, GR Supra 24.0%, RAV4 25.2%, Corolla Hatchback 25.5%, 4Runner 25.5%. Hybrids average 35.4% [H] — [TFLcar](https://tflcar.com/2026/03/used-car-depreciation-five-years-iseecars-study/)
- **iSeeCars 2026 worst five-year retention:** Nissan Leaf 63.1%, Infiniti QX80 62.8%, VW ID.4 62.1%, Tesla Model S 62.0%, Range Rover 61.7%, BMW 7 Series 61.6%, Model X 61.2%, Mach-E 60.8%, BMW 5 Series Hybrid 59.5%. Large luxury SUVs (Escalade, Navigator, Expedition) lose about 56–57% [H] — [TFLcar](https://tflcar.com/2026/03/used-car-depreciation-five-years-iseecars-study/)
- **iSeeCars 2025:** Jaguar I-Pace was worst at about 72.2–72.7% over 5 years. Porsche 911 lost 19.5% and 718 Cayman 21.8% [H] — [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/754572/cars-worst-depreciation-2025/)
- KBB: luxury, performance and less reliable brands depreciate faster; Toyota and Lexus have the lowest five-year cost to own [M] — [KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-depreciation)
- **Watches:** The 2022 peak and correction (see Q1) shows appreciation reversing. Bob's Watches and other dealers say a growing retail supply of Rolex reduces the secondary premium [L] — [The Diamond Box](https://thediamondbox.co.uk/the-rolex-secondary-market-in-2026-what-the-numbers-actually-tell-us)
- **Chanel:** Retail price for the Medium Classic Flap went $2,850 (2010) → $5,800 (2019) → $10,200 (2023) → $11,300 (Aug 2025, +4–5%). Prices have risen at least once a year since 2016 [M] — [Luxury Evermore](https://luxuryevermore.com/blogs/article/chanel-classic-flap-bag-price-history); [Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/understanding-the-latest-2025-chanel-bag-price-hikes-and-the-resale-market)
- Fashionphile: a Jumbo Flap bought for $6,400 (2019) was valued at about $12,200 six years later. The Chanel 25 resells for more than 80% above retail [L, platform marketing] — [Fashionphile](https://www.fashionphile.com/blogs/academy/chanel-resale-value-how-to-maximize-profits)
- Collaboration and limited editions drive spikes (LV Murakami 130–142%, Speedmaster Snoopy about +27%) [L/M] — see Q1 sources
- **Lab-grown risk:** Prices are still falling. Retailer opinion on whether they have stabilised is split [L] — [Liori Diamonds](https://lioridiamonds.com/blogs/news/lab-grown-diamond-value-the-honest-truth-about-resale-worth-in-2026)
- **Sneaker risk:** Higher Nike supply removed the scarcity premium. ASICS volume rose 45% YoY in 2025 as trends shifted [M] — [Retail-News.de](https://retail-news.de/stockx-resale-index-2025-2026/); [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/technology/stockx-spreads-as-it-melts/121887.article)

### Inferences
- Suggested brand multipliers relative to category baseline (my synthesis):
  - **Cars:** Porsche sports cars about 1.4–1.6× baseline retention. Toyota and Lexus, Honda about 1.2×. EVs and large luxury about 0.7×.
  - **Watches:** Rolex and Patek ≥1.0 of retail. Omega about 0.7. Fashion watches about 0.1–0.2 (unsourced).
  - **Bags:** Hermès about 1.3. Goyard about 1.3. Chanel and LV about 0.85–0.9. The Row about 0.95.
- Model appreciation only as an optional, user-confirmed flag, and cap it. Every documented appreciation run (watches 2020–22, sneakers 2020, natural diamonds pre-2022) was followed by a 20–60% correction.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-platform) audit of Rebag or TRR retention methodology. Both are self-reported.
- No smartwatch data was retrieved. Assumption: tracks consumer electronics, about 40–60% lost in year 1 (unsourced).

## Key Question 3: Seasonal, event and launch timing effects, and transaction specifics (fees, authentication, selling before repairs)

### Takeaway
For cars, spring and summer (March–August) are seller-favourable months and November–December are weakest. Selling before about 60,000–70,000 miles (warranty expiry, major service) and before the year 5 and year 8 repair-cost jumps is well supported. A "3-year optimum" is not established by any study I found. Luxury resale fees (about 20–50%) erase much of the paper retention.

### Cited Findings
- iSeeCars (2023–24 sales): the worst months for used-car buyers start in April and June is the worst month for deals, meaning the strongest prices for sellers. Business Insider's seller-side reading of earlier data: best months after April are May, June, July, August and March. November has the most deals, making it the weakest month to sell [M] — [Fox6/iSeeCars](https://www.fox6now.com/news/best-worst-times-buy-used-car); [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/the-best-times-of-year-to-sell-different-types-vehicles-2020-4/)
- The tax-refund season (Feb–Apr, US) effect was **not found** in a sourced study. It is consistent with the spring strength above, but treat it as an inference.
- Bumper analysis of Consumer Expenditure Survey data: annual repair costs jump 31.2% at year 5 and 59.4% at year 8, with a flat stretch in years 6–7 [M] — [Fool](https://www.fool.com/money/insurance/auto/articles/how-long-should-you-keep-a-car-to-get-the-most-for-your-money)
- Value takes another hit past 60,000 miles as warranties expire. Powertrain warranties end around 70,000 miles, which coincides with major scheduled maintenance [L/M] — [Lithia](https://www.lithia.com/research/car-selling/when-is-it-time-to-sell-your-car.htm); [AutoGuide](https://www.autoguide.com/auto/when-is-a-used-car-no-longer-worth-buying-44639122)
- Consumer guidance: years 3–6 are a good window to sell [L] — [Lithia](https://www.lithia.com/research/car-selling/when-is-it-time-to-sell-your-car.htm)
- **Luxury launch timing:** Chanel's annual (sometimes more frequent) retail increases lift the resale value of classics. The Aug 2025 increase was +4–5% [M] — [Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/understanding-the-latest-2025-chanel-bag-price-hikes-and-the-resale-market). A larger retail supply of Rolex compresses grey-market premiums [L] — [The Diamond Box](https://thediamondbox.co.uk/the-rolex-secondary-market-in-2026-what-the-numbers-actually-tell-us)
- **Consignment fees:** The RealReal's commission is tiered by item value and rewards level (third-party estimates: seller keeps about 20–85%). Examples: 40% payout for items ≤$145, 50% for $146–195; first-time consignors about 40–45%. Fashionphile: mainly buyout, up to about 70% on consignment. Example: a $3,000 bag gets about $1,500–2,000 on a Fashionphile buyout versus about $2,100–2,400 at TRR [L, blog sources] — [TRR FAQ](https://www.therealreal.com/faq/consign); [RetailBoss](https://retailboss.co/the-realreal-fashionphile-offer-distinct-advantages-60-billion-dollar-resale-market); [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/consignment-vs-buyout)
- 47% of shoppers consider resale value before buying new (TRR 2025) [M, platform] — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/5-ways-luxury-resale-is-reshaping-the-fashion-landscape/2025090883804)

### Inferences
- **App timing rules:**
  - Cars: flag "sell window" at year 3–5 or about 60k mi / 100k km, whichever comes first. Flag spring/summer as the best months and Nov–Dec as the worst.
  - Coats and other seasonal apparel: sell before the season starts (autumn for coats). This is industry common sense; I found no sourced dataset.
  - Watches: track the index, not the calendar.
- **Net-to-seller multiplier:** apply about 0.6–0.8 to platform "retention" for consigned luxury goods, about 0.5–0.75 of melt for gold sold locally, and about 0.85–0.95 for a private-party car sale versus trade-in (trade-in gap not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No sourced data for: US tax-refund season effect on prices; Australian seasonal car patterns (EOFY June, December); seasonal resale data for coats and outerwear; holiday-season jewellery resale timing.
- Authentication fees and rejection rates were not found. The platforms bundle authentication into their commission.
