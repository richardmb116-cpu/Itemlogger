# Home Appliances: Useful Life, Failure Rates, Repair-vs-Replace, and Resale Value

Scope: (a) major appliances (refrigerators, washers, dryers, dishwashers, ovens/ranges); (b) small appliances (vacuums: Dyson cordless vs corded/robot; coffee/espresso machines; microwaves; air fryers; blenders/stand mixers).
Confidence key: **H** = primary or large-sample data; **M** = reputable secondary source or a consistent rule of thumb; **L** = unsourced guide, anecdote, or commercial blog.
Geography: US = United States, UK, AU = Australia, EU = Belgium/Italy/Portugal/Spain consumer panels.

---

## Q1. Life expectancy and failure/repair curves by age

### Takeaway
Major appliances last about 9–15 years, and survey-based averages are rising slightly (EU large appliances averaged 12.5 yrs in 2023). Small appliances are much shorter-lived. Cordless stick vacuums average about 4.5 yrs, pod coffee machines about 4–5.5 yrs and espresso machines about 7 yrs, while corded vacuums last about 10 yrs. For refrigerators, roughly one-third need a repair by year 5 (Consumer Reports). First-year service rates at a large US retailer run about 4–9% for the best brands.

### Cited Findings
**Lifespan tables (rules of thumb / survey averages)**
- InterNACHI chart (US, home-inspector reference; undated, widely reproduced 2018–2025) gives: dryer 13 yrs; dishwasher 9; electric range 13–15; gas range 15–17; gas oven 10–18; microwave 9; refrigerator 9–13; washing machine 5–15; freezer 10–20; garbage disposal 12. Confidence **M** (expert consensus, not measured failure data). — [This Old House citing InterNACHI](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/home-finances/appliance-repair-statistics); [InterNACHI chart](https://www.nachi.org/life-expectancy.html)
- An industry guide (US, Southern California, early 2026) puts average and premium-brand lifespans as follows. Top-mount fridge 15–20 yrs. French-door fridge 12–15 yrs (Sub-Zero 18–22). Top-load washer 12–14 (Speed Queen 15+). Front-load washer 10–13 (Miele 14–16). Dryer 13–15 (premium 16–18). Dishwasher 10–13 (Miele/Bosch 15–18). Gas range 15–20 (Wolf/Viking 20–25). Electric range 13–16 (premium 18–22). Built-in microwave 9–12. The guide claims "warranty claim databases" but names none, so confidence is **L–M**. — [ServiceMag, Appliance Repair vs Replace (2026)](https://www.servicemag.org/guides/appliance-repair-vs-replace)
- EU survey data, 2019 to 2023 (Euroconsumers survey, Belgium/Italy/Portugal/Spain; published by the EEA in Dec 2024). The sample is not representative of the general population. Confidence **H** for relative ordering and **M** for absolute values.
  - Large household appliances: average lifespan 11 yrs 7 mo (2019) rising to 12 yrs 6 mo (2023), about +1.8%/yr.
  - Small household appliances: 8 yrs 8 mo rising to 9 yrs 1 mo.
  - Microwave: 9 yrs 10 mo. Espresso machine: 7 yrs 2 mo. Pod/capsule machine: about 5 yrs 6 mo.
  - Vacuums: cordless upright about 4 yrs 6 mo (shortest-lived vacuum type); robot vacuum about 1 yr longer (~5.5); corded cylinder/upright about 10 yrs.
  - Most small and large appliances are replaced for **reliability** reasons, while high-tech items are replaced because they are out of date.
  - Source: [EEA, Evolution in average lifespans of household appliances (2024)](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/circularity/sectoral-modules/product-lifespans/evolution-in-average-lifespans-of-household-appliances)
- Which? (UK) owner surveys:
  - Dishwashers and washing machines typically last about 10 yrs; fridge-freezers and tumble dryers about 11 yrs (2021). Confidence **M–H**.
  - Pod coffee machines had the shortest life of kitchen gadgets, at "just over four years". Kettles, irons, air fryers and traditional coffee machines were also near the bottom (2019). Confidence **M**.
  - Sources: [Which? 2021, Built to last](https://www.which.co.uk/news/2021/12/built-to-last-were-appliances-really-made-better-in-the-past); [Which? 2019, Most reliable kitchen gadgets](https://www.which.co.uk/news/2019/07/most-reliable-kitchen-gadgets-revealed/) (cited via search summaries)
- Canstar Blue (AU) consumer survey: owners keep a vacuum about 7 yrs before replacing. Consumer NZ expects about 8 yrs for a corded vacuum. Confidence **M**. — [Canstar Blue, Appliance life expectancy](https://www.canstarblue.com.au/appliances/appliances-life-expectancy/); Consumer NZ page (not fetchable from this session)

**Failure and repair rates by age**
- Consumer Reports (US) member surveys on refrigerators. Confidence **M–H**; the primary CR pages are paywalled and were seen via secondary summaries.
  - About 33% of all refrigerators need a repair by the end of year 5 of ownership.
  - About 31% of fridges with icemakers have a problem by year 5.
  - Older CR data: side-by-side fridges with icemakers had 31% needing repair by year 4 (2014). "Nearly half" of fridges sold since 2015 had some issue within 5 years.
  - Sources: [CR Spanish page](https://espanol.consumerreports.org/appliances/refrigerators/most-and-least-reliable-refrigerator-brands-a8271265835); [BGR summary](https://bgr.com/2115888/most-reliable-refrigerator-brand-consumer-reports)
- CR's 2026 brand reliability ratings are defined as the likelihood a new appliance breaks or stops working **within the first 5 years**. In Oct 2026 CR launched a "#1 in Reliability" badge for kitchen appliances. — [CR press release, Oct 2026](https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2026/10/consumer-reports-launches-new-1-in-reliability-badge-to-help-shoppers-find-long-lasting-kitchen-appliances/)
- Which? (UK) survey, July 2025: over 9,000 respondents covering about 28,000 appliances. It reports cumulative fault rates by brand. Confidence **H** for the existence of a brand spread; brand names were not extracted.
  - Dishwashers: 7% (best brand) vs 33% (worst) faulty in the first 6 years.
  - Corded vacuums: 8% vs 37% faulty in the first 7 years.
  - Washer-dryers: 35% faulty within 7 years for one brand.
  - Hobs: 4% in 7 years for the best brand. Freezers: 0% in 7 years for the best brand.
  - Source: [Which?, The appliances that last the longest (Dec 2025)](https://www.which.co.uk/news/2020/12/the-appliances-that-last-the-longest/)
- Yale Appliance (US, Greater Boston retailer) **first-year** service rate, defined as units serviced in year 1 divided by units sold. Based on 33,190 service calls; published Dec 2025 (2026 list). Confidence **M**: retailer self-reported and limited to brands it sells, but a large sample.
  - Brand rates: Speed Queen 3.7%; LG 5.5%; Miele 7.4% (Miele dishwashers 5.5%, down from 10%); Sharp 7.5%; Gaggenau 7.7%; Bosch Benchmark 7.8%; GE Profile 8.0%; LG Studio 8.2%; Bosch 8.7%; Signature Kitchen Suite 8.8%; GE 9.2%.
  - French-door fridges (icemakers) and pro ranges (igniters) are the most serviced categories. Laundry and dishwashers hold up best.
  - Source: [Yale Appliance, Most Reliable Appliance Brands 2026](https://blog.yaleappliance.com/bid/86332/The-Least-Serviced-Most-Reliable-Appliance-Brands-Reviews-Ratings)
- CHOICE (AU) fridges, 2021 survey of more than 7,100 members: 70% had no problems with a fridge bought in the previous 8 years. Common faults were broken drawers/shelves (6%) and noise (5%). Confidence **M–H**. — [CHOICE fridge reliability](https://choice.com.au/home-and-living/kitchen/fridges/articles/fridges-reliability)
- CHOICE 2024 washing machine brand satisfaction: Miele 82%, Bosch 79% and LG 79% (front loaders); LG 72% and Fisher & Paykel 70% (top loaders). Confidence **M**. — [CHOICE washing machine reliability](https://img.choice.com.au/home-and-living/laundry-and-cleaning/washing-machines/articles/washing-machine-reliability)
- US repair demand: in a 2,000-homeowner survey, 54% said a refrigerator was the appliance they most recently repaired or replaced. Ovens/ranges/cooktops were next at 22%, then dishwashers at 7%. Confidence **L–M**. — [This Old House, appliance repair statistics](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/home-finances/appliance-repair-statistics)
- Norway long-run data: washer lifetimes fell from 19.2 to 10.6 years over a 70-year household dataset. This was cited as a related article teaser and not verified. Confidence **L**. — [ServiceMag](https://www.servicemag.org/guides/appliance-repair-vs-replace)

### Inferences
- **Suggested useful-life parameters** (median; range):

  | Item | Median life | Range |
  |---|---|---|
  | Refrigerator | 12 yrs | 9–15 (top-mount longer, French-door with icemaker shorter) |
  | Washer | 11 yrs | 10–14 (Speed Queen/Miele 14–16+) |
  | Dryer | 13 yrs | 11–15 |
  | Dishwasher | 10 yrs | 9–13 (Miele/Bosch 15) |
  | Gas range | 15 yrs | 13–20 |
  | Electric range | 14 yrs | 13–16 |
  | Microwave | 9 yrs | 9–10 |
  | Corded vacuum | 9 yrs | 8–10 |
  | Robot vacuum | 5 yrs | 4–6 |
  | Cordless stick vacuum | 4.5 yrs | 4–6 (battery-limited) |
  | Espresso machine (pump/semi-auto, e.g. Breville/De'Longhi) | 7 yrs | 5–10 |
  | Pod machine (Nespresso) | 5 yrs | 4–6 |
  | Air fryer | 3–5 yrs | inferred from Which? "bottom of table"; no hard number |
  | Stand mixer (KitchenAid) | 10+ yrs | inferred; no survey number |

- **Hazard curve:** about 5–9% needing service in year 1 (Yale; includes installation and infant-mortality issues) and about 30–33% cumulative by year 5 for fridges (CR). Year-1 service is concentrated in installation/infant mortality, then failures run at roughly a flat ~5–6%/yr through midlife and rise after about 70–80% of expected life. A Weibull shape parameter of about 1.5–2.5 for major appliances is a reasonable modelling assumption, not a sourced figure.
- **End-of-life onset:** about 70–80% of median life (e.g. year 8 for a fridge or dishwasher, year 10–11 for a dryer, year 3–3.5 for a cordless stick vacuum).

### Gaps
- The NAHB/Bank of America "Study of Life Expectancy of Home Components" (2007) was not retrieved. It is old and largely superseded by InterNACHI-style charts.
- No published age-specific annual hazard curves (e.g. % failing in year 3 vs year 7) were found for any appliance class. Only cumulative 5–7-year figures exist.
- No survey lifespan figures were found for air fryers, blenders or KitchenAid stand mixers.
- The CHOICE 2026 survey (5,012 respondents, including stick vacuums) results were not retrieved.

## Q2. Repair costs vs replacement; the 50% rule and age; cordless vacuum batteries

### Takeaway
Typical US repair visits cost $100–$450. The "50% rule" is the common heuristic, but Consumer Reports has replaced it with tools that weigh price, age and repair cost. Combine repair cost with the share of life used: repair if under about 30% of new price and under about 70% of life; replace if over about 50% of new price and over about 80% of life. Dyson cordless batteries are the main end-of-life driver for stick vacuums: a 2-yr guarantee, a realistic 2–4-yr life, and $70–$150 for a genuine replacement.

### Cited Findings
- **US repair cost ranges (Angi, 2025):** refrigerator $200–$300; oven/cooktop/range $100–$500; dishwasher $160–$300; washer $50–$450; dryer $100–$400. The HomeAdvisor/Angi cost guide gives similar ranges, with an oven average of $220 and a dryer average of $180. Fixr gives a general average of $200 (range $100–$300, Jan 2025). Confidence **M** (commercial lead-gen sites, but consistent with each other). — [Angi appliance repair](https://www.angi.com/nearme/appliance-repair); [Angi/HomeAdvisor cost guide](https://match.angi.com/cost/kitchens/repair-an-appliance); [Fixr](https://www.fixr.com/costs/kitchen-appliance-repair)
- Service call fees mostly run $75–$125 per visit, and homeowner dissatisfaction rises above $150 (This Old House 2025 survey). Confidence **M**. — [This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/home-finances/appliance-repair-statistics)
- **Worked repair examples (US, SoCal, early 2026):**
  - 8-yr top-load washer transmission: $475 vs $750 new (63%), so replace.
  - 3-yr Samsung fridge icemaker: $275–$400 vs $2,200 (about 15%), so repair.
  - 12-yr dryer heating element: $200–$265 vs $700–$900.
  - 11-yr front-load spider arm: $350–$500 vs $1,100, often followed by a $350–$500 bearing job.
  - Sub-Zero sealed system: $800–$1,500 vs $9,000–$12,000.
  - Wolf range control board: $1,200 vs $6,500 (about 18%).
  - Confidence **L–M**. — [ServiceMag (2026)](https://www.servicemag.org/guides/appliance-repair-vs-replace)
- **50% rule and age modifiers.** These are rules of thumb, not data. Confidence **M** as heuristics.
  - Consumer Reports' former guidance: replace if repair is over 50% of a new unit's price. Its updated guidance, based on member data covering 528,000 products bought 2012–2022, uses interactive tools that weigh purchase price, age and repair cost. CR also says that if a product has already been repaired once, even 49% may be too much. — [MoneyCrashers on CR guidance](https://www.moneycrashers.com/appliance-repair-50-percent-rule/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/when-to-repair-or-replace-your-appliances)
  - Common variant: replace if the appliance is past 50% of expected life **and** repair is over 50% of new. — [MoneyCrashers](https://www.moneycrashers.com/appliance-repair-50-percent-rule/)
  - ServiceMag four-factor test: repair if under 30% of replacement cost and under 70% of lifespan; replace if over 50% and over 80% of lifespan. Use a 35% threshold for premium appliances over $3,000. Under 5 yrs old, usually repair. Past 75% of life with only aftermarket parts, lean toward replacement. "Sentinel failures" (washer spider arm or transmission; compressor failure with a corroded evaporator) mean replace. — [ServiceMag](https://www.servicemag.org/guides/appliance-repair-vs-replace)
- **Dyson cordless batteries:**
  - Dyson gives a 2-year guarantee on cordless machines including the battery (AU, SG), and a 1-year guarantee on batteries bought as spare parts. Confidence **H** (manufacturer). — [Dyson AU replacement batteries](https://dyson.com.au/support/replacement-batteries); [Dyson SG](https://www.dyson.com.sg/support/replacement-battery)
  - The UK V11 replacement battery page says the part is guaranteed 12 months. — [Dyson UK](https://www.dyson.co.uk/vacuum-cleaners/accessories/replacement-battery/v11)
  - Genuine replacement battery costs about $70–$150 (US); third-party $30–$80; forum reports of $150–$200 out of warranty. Third-party batteries may void the warranty. Confidence **L–M**. — [SmartHomeStarter Dyson battery guide](https://smarthomestarter.com/dyson-vacuum-battery-guide-everything-you-need-to-know); [Dyson community](https://community.dyson.com/ask-the-community-49/must-we-continually-pay-to-replace-our-dyson-every-1-2-years-3053)
  - Battery life estimates (unsourced guides and anecdotes): 3–4 yrs at moderate use, 2–3 yrs with heavy daily use. User reports of failure at about 2–3.5 yrs. Confidence **L**. — [SmartHomeStarter](https://smarthomestarter.com/dyson-vacuum-battery-guide-everything-you-need-to-know); [sikayetvar complaint](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/dyson-us/dyson-v15-battery-failed-after-35-years-with-no-warning)
  - In Dec 2025 a US law firm announced an *investigation* (not a finding) into V11/V15 premature battery failure and run-time claims. — [ClassLawDC (Dec 2025)](https://classlawdc.com/2025/12/04/dyson-v11-v15-cordless-vacuums-battery-investigation)
- **Energy cost as a repair-decision factor:** pre-2010 fridges use 40–60% more electricity, about $75–$120/yr extra (rule of thumb, **L–M**). — [ServiceMag](https://www.servicemag.org/guides/appliance-repair-vs-replace)

### Inferences
- **App rule (suggested).** Let `life_frac = age / median_life`. Set the repair threshold to 50% of current new price when `life_frac` is under 0.5, and drop it linearly to 30% at `life_frac` = 0.8 and to about 15% beyond 1.0. Lower it by a further 10 points if the unit has had a previous major repair. For units over $3,000 new (Sub-Zero/Wolf/Miele), raise it to about 35–50% even late in life, because their parts support and lifespans are longer.
- A cordless stick vacuum at 2–3 yrs whose battery has faded is a cheap repair (a battery costs about 15–25% of a new $500–$750 Dyson). The economics favour a battery swap once, but not a second time at year 5–6 when motor and plastics are also aging.
- For major appliances, the service call fee ($75–$150) is a fixed floor, so cheap appliances (a $100 microwave, a $80 air fryer) are almost never worth professional repair. Model small appliances under $150 as "replace on failure".

### Gaps
- No primary Consumer Reports thresholds by age were retrievable (the pages are paywalled).
- iFixit repairability scores and repair costs for espresso machines (Breville/De'Longhi descaling, pump, thermoblock) were not retrieved.
- UK/AU repair cost benchmarks were not found.
- Dyson's official US battery price and cycle-life spec were not found (Dyson does not publish cycle counts).

## Q3. Second-hand resale value: share of new price, age effects, brand retention

### Takeaway
There is **no rigorous published study** of used-appliance prices. The evidence is rules of thumb plus scattered eBay sold listings. Used major appliances typically fetch about 20–50% of original retail. A rough "50% off" average applies to near-new units, and Consumer Reports says older units can be up to 75% off. Premium brands (Sub-Zero, Wolf, Miele) are claimed to retain 50–70%, versus 20–40% for mainstream brands. KitchenAid stand mixers hold value unusually well (about 40–60%). Breville espresso machines retain meaningful value on eBay, but no percentages could be computed.

### Cited Findings
- Used kitchen appliances sell for about 20–50% of original retail, depending on condition, age, brand and features. Confidence **L** (unsourced guide). — [Home Essentials Guide](https://homeessentialsguide.pages.dev/how-much-to-sell-used-kitchen-appliances-for)
- Used appliances average about 50% off. Consumer Reports says older pre-owned appliances can be as much as 75% off the original retail price. Confidence **M**. — [Yahoo / Consumer Reports, Used appliances: should you buy one?](https://news.yahoo.com/used-appliances-buy-one-144437620.html); [CR original (paywalled)](https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/used-appliances-should-you-buy-one)
- Luxury brands (Sub-Zero, Wolf, Miele, Viking) are claimed to retain 50–70% of retail, versus 20–40% for standard brands (Whirlpool, GE, Frigidaire). A calculator site claims 20–40% of original value after 5 years. Confidence **L** (unsourced commercial tools). — [ItemValueChecker appliances](https://itemvaluechecker.com/appliances); [Appliance depreciation calculator](https://calc-template.pages.dev/everyday-life/appliance-depreciation-calculator)
- A washer with uneven drum rotation or a loud motor is claimed to lose 15–30% of resale value. Confidence **L**. — [EngineerFix](https://engineerfix.com/how-much-is-a-used-washer-worth/)
- Enthusiast forum discussion notes **low** resale value on used Speed Queen washers despite their reliability. This suggests high reliability does not translate into a resale premium in local marketplaces. Confidence **L** (anecdotal). — [automaticwasher.org thread](https://automaticwasher.org/threads/low-resale-value-on-used-speed-queen-washers-and-dryers.72361/post-956385)
- KitchenAid stand mixers: a used Professional ($400–500 new) sells for $200–350, and a used KitchenAid in good order holds about 40–60% of retail. Individual eBay sales go much lower (e.g. a Classic K45SS sold for $80.99). Confidence **L–M**. — [ItemValueChecker kitchen items](https://itemvaluechecker.com/kitchen-items); [Underpriced flipping guide](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/kitchen-appliances-flipping-guide); [eBay listing](https://www.ebay.com/itm/156946920383)
- Breville espresso, individual eBay sold prices (US): a used Oracle Touch sold for $636 (the Oracle Touch new price is typically about $2,500–$3,000, from general knowledge and not verified here); a Barista Pro sold for $800 (high, possibly new-in-box); a Barista Touch average used price was $409. Confidence **L** (single transactions). — [eBay coffee/espresso category](https://www.ebay.com/t/KitchenAid-Coffee-Tea-Espresso-Makers/38250/bn_2702639)

### Inferences
These are modelling parameters inferred from the above, all **L–M** confidence. Treat them as priors and calibrate against eBay sold data if possible.

| Sub-category | Yr-1 drop (private sale vs new price) | Annual decline thereafter | Floor (share of new) | Value cliff onset |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- No UK/AU-specific resale data was found.
- No data was found on Dyson or Nespresso resale percentages.
- The Breville percentage-of-retail could not be computed from the retrieved data.

## Q4. Seasonal timing, moving-related selling, and energy-efficiency upgrade economics

### Takeaway
New-appliance discounting peaks in September–October (new model year for washers, dryers and dishwashers) and around Black Friday and US holiday weekends. Fridges are the exception: new models arrive in summer, so May is "last call". Replacing a refrigerator 15+ years old saves about $50–$150/yr in electricity (Energy Star). That is real, but rarely enough on its own to justify early replacement of a working mid-life fridge.

### Cited Findings
- Lowe's merchandising VP: new models are typically released in September and October for washers, dryers and dishwashers, and older stock is discounted to clear space. Fridge makers roll out new models in summer, making May the last call for old-model deals. Confidence **M** (retailer-quoted, via affiliate press). — [Kiplinger, Best times to buy household appliances](https://www.kiplinger.com/article/spending/T050-C011-S001-best-times-to-buy-household-appliances.html); [Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/articles/best-time-to-buy-appliances/)
- Black Friday events now run multiple weeks with cuts on nearly all major appliances. Labor Day, Memorial Day, Presidents' Day and July 4 weekends are smaller US sale events. Confidence **M** (affiliate-driven sources, so a commercial bias). — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/home/best-time-to-buy-appliances); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/homeowners/when-to-buy-appliances.html)
- Energy Star and EPA "Flip Your Fridge" guidance:
  - Recycle fridges 15+ yrs old and replace them with an Energy Star model.
  - Removing an old second fridge saves up to $50/yr.
  - Replacing and recycling saves about $150, or "more than $220" in a later fact sheet, over a 12-year product lifetime.
  - Confidence **H** (government). — [EPA press release](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epa/newsreleases/epas-energy-star-launches-flip-your-fridge-initiative.html); [Energy Star FYF fact sheet 2021](https://www.energystar.gov/sites/default/files/tools/02162021_FYF_factsheet_v1.pdf); [Energy Star refrigerators](https://www.energystar.gov/products/refrigerators)
- A 20-year-old fridge uses about 1,700 kWh/yr vs about 450 kWh/yr for a new Energy Star model of similar size: about $150/yr at $0.12/kWh. Confidence **M**. — [Clean Energy Resource Teams (MN)](https://www.cleanenergyresourceteams.org/your-old-refrigerator-energy-hog)
- Many retailers pick up and recycle old fridges on purchase. Many US utilities and municipalities pay recycling incentives. — [Energy Star refrigerators](https://www.energystar.gov/products/refrigerators); [Mass Save](https://masssave.com/blog/residential/adding-up-the-benefits-of-over-ten-years-of-appliance-recycling)

### Inferences
- **Selling-timing parameters (inferred):**
  - The used-appliance supply spike tracks the moving season: May–September in the US/UK, and Dec–Feb in AU, where the peak is the summer/new-year lease turnover. Fridges and washers often don't move with renters, so supply rises and asking prices soften by an assumed 5–15% at peak. This is unsourced, so confidence **L**.
  - Buying new is cheapest in Sept–Oct and on Black Friday (major appliances) and around Black Friday and Prime Day (small appliances: Dyson, Breville, Nespresso, KitchenAid). These dates also depress used prices for the same models.
- **Upgrade economics:**
  - A replacement fridge ($1,000–$1,500) paid for by roughly $100–$150/yr energy savings has a payback of about 8–15 yrs. Energy alone justifies replacement only for 20-yr-plus units or second "garage" fridges. Elsewhere it is a tiebreaker in repair decisions (add about $75–$150/yr to the cost of keeping a pre-2010 fridge).
  - Washers and dishwashers: water and energy savings are smaller. No hard figure was retrieved.

## Q5. Obsolescence and standards changes affecting value

### Takeaway
Major appliances lose value mainly through wear and failure risk, not technological obsolescence. Large appliances are "replaced for reliability reasons" (EEA). The exceptions are smart/connected appliances, which depend on manufacturer software support, and battery-powered small appliances, where battery availability defines life.

### Cited Findings
- Most small and large household appliances are replaced for reliability reasons. High-tech devices (except printers) are replaced for non-reliability reasons such as being outdated. Confidence **H**. — [EEA (2024)](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/circularity/sectoral-modules/product-lifespans/evolution-in-average-lifespans-of-household-appliances)
- Which? has investigated how long smart appliances receive security updates, flagging that connected features can lose support well before the hardware fails. Confidence **M**; the article was identified in search but its specific figures were not extracted. — [Which?, The truth behind smart appliance security updates](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-truth-behind-smart-appliance-security-updates-aERdx7b1sdHq)
- Energy Star and federal efficiency standards make pre-2010/pre-2001 fridges materially less efficient (40–60% more electricity), which depresses their economic value. — [ServiceMag](https://www.servicemag.org/guides/appliance-repair-vs-replace); [Energy Star](https://www.energystar.gov/products/refrigerators)
- After the warranty, parts support drives repairability. Past about 75% of life with only aftermarket parts available, guides recommend replacement. Confidence **L–M**. — [ServiceMag](https://www.servicemag.org/guides/appliance-repair-vs-replace)

### Inferences
- Apply an extra obsolescence factor to connected appliances: a 3–5% per-year additional decline after about 5 years, when app support is uncertain. Do the same for robot vacuums, given fast model churn and app dependence. Do not apply one to mechanical items (KitchenAid mixer, corded vacuums, gas ranges).
- For battery appliances (Dyson cordless), tie value to battery age rather than unit age if the app records a battery replacement date. A fresh genuine battery should reset part of the value decline (assume +10–15 points).

### Gaps
- Refrigerant transitions (US AIM Act HFC phase-down, R-134a to R-600a) and their effect on repair costs or used-fridge value were not researched in this pass.
- EU/UK right-to-repair rules (spare-parts availability obligations of 7–10 years for washers, dishwashers and fridges under EU ecodesign rules) were not verified in this pass.
- No specific Which? figures for software-support periods were extracted.
- No data was found on how Dyson discontinuing battery models affects resale.
